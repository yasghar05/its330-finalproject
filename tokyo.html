Tokyo

Historical Overview

Tokyo was at first a small fishing village called Edo, but in 1603 everything changed. Around that time, it became head of the Tokugawa shogunate, leading its dawn into becoming one of the biggest cities in the world. About 255 years later in 1868, the small fishing village now turned large-scale city was renamed into Tokyo itself, which literally means Eastern Capital, and it became so for the country of Japan. Throughout much more significant cultural events, such as World War II, the city only kept growing, allowing for itself to become the tech, finance, and culture giant that everyone knows it for today.

  2. Senso-ji Temple

  5. Tokyo Skytree

Cultural Significance (Why Is It Heavily Visited?)

Tokyo's is Japan's capital city that blends in the old with the new of standard customs that we all know Japan for of shrines, festivals, and sumo wrestlers with the new pop culture fads of anime, fashion, music, and technology. In other words, Tokyo city embraces contemporary global trends while still respecting older harmonious, disciplinary values. So, if you like a place that is old meets new, highly routed with food, shopping, and effective public transportation with endless entertaining experiences and prevalent cultural landmarks, then Japan may be the place for you.
